# Fresh visual review: voice boundary timing

Inspected the complete entrance.png and exit.png figures plus full-resolution boundary-region crops. Target: timing plots should support comparisons of signal timing and spectral structure around a clearly identified zero, with readable physical units and honest shared scales. This review uses images only; it provides no listening evidence or judgment of audibility/naturalness.

## Findings

- **Minor ambiguity, high confidence:** Both edited rows in both figures show two closely spaced green vertical markers near zero, but the figures provide no legend or label distinguishing them. The titles explain the zero boundary; the second marker's meaning and interval cannot be determined from the image. Label the second marker or use a keyed boundary band with its duration if the distinction matters.
- **Cross-figure comparison caveat, high confidence:** Entrance waveform limits are ±0.100, while exit limits are approximately ±0.050. This is honestly labeled and the heading explicitly promises shared scales across rows, so it is not a within-figure scale defect. Nevertheless, equal apparent waveform height across the two figures does not represent equal amplitude. A small note would help if these are presented together for amplitude comparison.
- **Fine-time-resolution limitation, high confidence:** Approximately 700 ms occupy each panel, with labeled ticks every 100 ms. This is clear for broad entrance timing differences, but the tightly spaced boundary markers and individual near-boundary waveform samples are not distinguishable enough for a precise few-millisecond transition judgment. An additional local inset would be appropriate if that precision is claimed.

## Readability and comparability

Both figures are readable at full size. Row labels, output-frame timestamps, milliseconds relative to boundary, amplitude full-scale units, frequency in kHz, and power-spectral-density colorbar units are present. No material label collision or clipping is visible. The frequency range and spectral color mapping match across rows and figures. Waveforms share limits within each figure and do not visibly hit their upper/lower limits. All rows use comparable time framing and align their zero markers. Spectral structures remain visible against the dark background; black regions alone should not be interpreted as literal silence because the color scale has a displayed floor.

## Verdict

Usable visual timing evidence with minor annotation improvements. The most concrete presentation issue is the unexplained second green marker. These images alone cannot verify audio quality, continuity, or naturalness.
