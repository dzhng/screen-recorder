# Independent visual review of Apple-decoded recording output

Reviewed 2026-09-28. No implementation files, numerical measurement reports, encoder settings or output sizes were read. This is visual evidence, not preset acceptance or a default-selection decision.

## Target and comparison

The encoded recording should preserve the captured screen's readable characters, thin grid and UI borders, small colored icons, pointer shape and trail state at the matching frame. Compression must not invent breaks or additional edges in the grid, obscure text, or leave a trail absent from the matching reference.

Inspected all 48 supplied PNGs: opening, motion and pointer cohorts, four reference frames (0, 10, 20, 29) and all three matching decoded candidates. All opening/motion images are 1920×1080; layouts/content matched per frame. The pointer set also showed matching content and framing within each comparison. Opening is effectively static; motion includes a scroll between frames 10 and 20; pointer includes a visible pink trail that fades between frames 10 and 20 and is mostly gone by 29.

Comparison sheets used labels A=balanced, B=sharp, C=compact. Labels were neutral on sheets, though this was not a fully blinded trial because I generated the mapping. I did not use preset names as quality evidence. Initial overview sheets for opening/motion were downsampled JPEGs, so fine-detail conclusions use lossless PNG crops enlarged with nearest-neighbor, not those overviews. Pointer overview sheets were PNG. Artifacts are in `/tmp/quality-independent/`.

## Visible findings

- **Thin pale grid lines provide the strongest separation.** At opening frame 0, compact has broken/faded horizontal stretches, doubled pale edges and uneven vertical lines. These are visible in the full-frame view and clearly visible at 2× in `opening-0-grid.png`. Balanced has weaker instances of uneven line strength, with some vertical segments washed out. Sharp is closest to the reference's continuous regular grid in this crop, though it is not identical. The pointer cohort's frame 0 reproduces the conspicuous compact grid degradation. Confidence: high for compact's loss, moderate for sharp over balanced.
- **The initial grid loss substantially settles in later frames.** In `grid-temporal.png`, frames 10, 20 and 29 look much more regular than frame 0 across candidates. Compact retains some unevenness, but the large first-frame defects are reduced. The reference grid itself remains steady. This is evidence of changing reconstruction quality during the static opening; sampled stills cannot establish the perceptual severity of flicker in playback.
- **Text is readable in all candidates.** The submenu heading, instructional paragraph, Products button, visual token, motion heading and fine section labels remain recognizable. At 2×, encoded character contours are a little softened/changed compared with reference; compact's token edges are slightly rougher in `motion-29-token.png`. I saw no missing letters, substituted token characters or gross text smear in the sampled frames. I cannot support a strong balanced-versus-sharp legibility winner from these text crops. Confidence: high for preserved readability of sampled text, low for a broad typography ranking.
- **Colored icons and filled markers retain their identity.** Small saturated browser icons show softened color boundaries in all encoded versions. Compact appears slightly less clean around some high-contrast icon edges, but the crop is small and this is a weak separation. The orange motion target retains its circular border and white cross; no consequential shape or hue loss is apparent. The dark teal token/button and pale landmark cards remain distinguishable. Confidence: moderate for preserved identity, low for ranking subtle icon differences.
- **Scroll state is correct in the sampled motion frames.** Frames 20 and 29 show the lower page with the motion and sound cards, matching their references in all candidates. No gross displaced region, stale previous scroll position or duplicate text appears. The orange target stays at the left in the shown references and candidates: this cohort is evidence for screen scrolling, not for an animated moving target traversing the lane.
- **Actual pointer and trail state survive in every candidate.** `pointer-detail.png` shows frames 10, 20 and 29 at 3×. The black/white pointer silhouette and pink trail have the matching position and fade state. Encoded pink edges are a little softer, with slight fringe/halo changes; none removes the pointer or visibly leaves the long old trail at frame 29. Differences among presets here are small, with no defensible categorical winner. Confidence: high for correct sampled pointer/trail state, moderate for the absence of a large local artifact.

## Judgment and limits

For fidelity alone, the reference retains cleaner fine detail than all encoded candidates. Sharp is visually closer on the most discriminating first-frame grid crop; balanced is close on ordinary text and pointer detail; compact has the most conspicuous thin-line loss. This does not establish which speed/size/quality tradeoff should ship, and it does not justify a default change by itself.

This is a 12-timepoint still-frame inspection across three short cohorts, not continuous video playback, OCR measurement, a broad content corpus or a cross-decoder study. It cannot rule out transient inter-frame artifacts, prove long-run stability, evaluate audio, or generalize to dense code, photographs, dark interfaces or tiny type beyond these samples. No numerical reports were consulted after the visual judgment either.

## Extension: 10-second source interval 35–45 seconds

Reviewed all 16 images in `/tmp/screenrec-output-quality-long-2`: reference and each decoded candidate at frames 0, 100, 200 and 299. All are 1920×1080. Used lossless full-frame PNG sheets, native full-frame reference/compact views at frame 299, and nearest-neighbor enlarged lossless crops for the grid, token, final paragraph and horizontal rule. The same A/B/C mapping applies. No measurements, settings, output sizes or implementation were read.

- **Initial grid differences repeat over this longer encode.** At frame 0, compact again has conspicuous doubled/washed-out grid segments and uneven thickness. Balanced has a few weakened vertical segments; sharp is visually closest to the reference's regular lines in the selected crop. Confidence: high for compact degradation, moderate for sharp versus balanced. See `long-0-grid.png`.
- **By frame 100 the grid is much cleaner in all three.** The initial compact breaks/doubling have largely resolved, and differences are subtle in `long-100-grid.png`. This supports improvement after the first sampled frame, not a claim about precisely when it settles or whether it oscillates between samples.
- **Content changes and scroll positions match.** The token changes from SQQKAS at frame 0 to V4HETB by frame 100 in reference and every candidate. Frame 200 shows the lower motion/sound cards; frame 299 shows the first and second numbered text passages. I see no stale page section, repeated strip or incorrect sampled scroll offset. Confidence: high for these four timepoints.
- **Post-scroll text remains readable.** At frame 299, the heading “The first paragraph,” explanatory body and “A sentence to identify” remain legible in every candidate. Enlarged crops show slight contour/contrast changes relative to reference, but no evident missing character, substantial smear or clear balanced/sharp reading advantage. Compact also retains readable body text. The pale horizontal separator remains continuous in all three. Confidence: high for sampled text readability; low for ranking subtle text-edge differences.
- **Colored token and controls remain intact.** At frame 200, V4HETB and Change token stay distinguishable with matching fills and geometry; all encoded versions slightly soften/alter fine letter edges. No major color shift or lost control boundary is visible. Confidence: moderate.

The extension is consistent with the earlier visual conclusion: compact's first-frame thin-line damage is the strongest defect, while later sampled text screens offer little categorical separation. It adds evidence across a longer duration and multiple page positions but is still four sparse still samples, not playback inspection of the entire scrolling transition. No claim of smooth motion, frame pacing, absence of transient scroll artifacts or preset promotion follows from this review. Total inspection now covers 64 supplied images across four cohorts.
