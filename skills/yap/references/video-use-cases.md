# Video use cases

Read this reference when the requested deliverable is a launch video, demo,
podcast edit, episode introduction, teaser, trailer, tutorial or short social
clip. These are story shapes and review priorities, not presets. Choose one
from the user's purpose and material, then adapt it.

## Evidence-first choices

Use the transcript as the primary map for speech-led material. Pack or summarize
phrases for navigation, but return to exact word occurrences and source/project
clocks before cutting. Use frames, filmstrips and audio excerpts only at decision
points: a possible hook, a take comparison, an ambiguous boundary, a reaction,
or a proposed ending. Do not dump every frame into the agent's context.

Audio usually gives the cleanest cut candidates. Start near word boundaries and
silence gaps, then inspect picture and sound together. Preserve laughter, breaths,
emphasis, reactions and speaker handoffs when they carry the beat. A cut that is
clean in the transcript can still be wrong in the room tone, facial reaction or
visual continuity.

Before delivery, review the rendered output at every changed boundary, plus the
opening, ending and representative middle. Look for flashes, jumps, clipped
words, clicks, duplicated speech, hidden captions, unreadable titles and an
ending that gives away or fails to deliver the intended payoff. Fix the observed
defect, render again and recheck the changed behavior; stop after a bounded number
of useful passes and report what remains unresolved.

## Route by deliverable

Use the focused reference that owns the story decisions, then return here for the
shared evidence-first and delivery checks:

- [Launch and demo videos](launch-videos.md) owns one product promise, proof beats,
  reference-conditioned treatments and truthful calls to action.
- [Podcast and interview videos](podcast-videos.md) owns complete exchanges,
  multicamera/raw-source choices, speaker evidence and episode introductions.
- [Teasers and episode cold opens](teaser-videos.md) owns the open loop, including
  the optional cut immediately after a complete provocative question.

These references keep use cases separate so a launch demo does not inherit a
teaser's withheld answer or a podcast highlight lose its consequence. [Creative
workflows](creative-workflows.md) owns shared assembly techniques, while
[editorial checks](editorial-checks.md) owns rendered-output verification.

## Tutorials, walkthroughs and updates

Use a task-shaped arc:

`goal → setup → steps → gotcha or decision → result → recap`

Show the state before an action, the action itself and the resulting state. Keep
cursor or screen movement readable, remove navigation that does not teach the task,
and preserve the one exception or failure that prevents a viewer from copying the
wrong behavior. For a short update, compress to `what changed → why it matters →
what to do next`; do not bury the requested action under a general status report.

## Short social clips

Choose one beat rather than shrinking a full episode. Open with the claim, tension
or surprising image; keep one supporting exchange or demonstration; end on a
payoff, open question or next action that matches the brief. Design captions for
phone-size reading and review the first and last seconds at normal speed. A clip
may be fast, but do not trade away word boundaries, speaker meaning or the visual
cause of a result just to add more beats.

## Shape selection

The user's purpose determines the ending:

| Deliverable | Ending | Must protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch/demo | Benefit and next action | Proof of the promise |
| Full podcast/highlight | Answer and consequence | Qualifications and meaning |
| Episode introduction | Transition into episode | Curiosity without repetition |
| Teaser/trailer | Open question and truthful destination | Complete question; no accidental answer |
| Tutorial/update | Result, recap or next action | Causal steps and the useful exception |
| Social clip | Payoff, open loop or action | One coherent beat |

When the brief does not specify a shape, inspect the material and choose the
smallest shape that gives the viewer a clear reason to continue or act.
