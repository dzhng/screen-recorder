# Podcast and interview videos

Read this reference for a full podcast highlight, a multicamera interview edit,
or an episode introduction. It owns the story and source-selection decisions;
the public operation contracts and focused acoustic, picture and caption checks
remain in the references linked by [video-use-cases](video-use-cases.md).

## Start with the capability question

Before choosing a workflow, ask: **what tools, skills, models and asset libraries
are available beyond the Yap skill and product?** Save a relevant inventory with
provider, readiness and provenance. Use available tools and recommend a missing
external capability only when the brief materially requires it. Do not install
one by default. Prepare registered Yap models through their normal product
operation when needed.

## Preserve the exchange

For a self-contained highlight, protect:

`question or setup → answer → consequence`

Read the complete exchange before cutting. Keep qualifications, reactions,
laughter, breaths and handoffs that carry meaning. Remove dead air or false
starts only within the brief, and preserve a complete answer when a shorter
fragment would lose the point. A dark or suspenseful episode introduction can
escalate through a provocative question, contrasting positions and a reaction,
then transition into the episode; it must not repeat an answer without a clear
reason.

## Choose sources and clocks deliberately

Compare raw camera takes for the same beat rather than concatenating files in
directory order. Prefer raw per-person sources when they support the requested
shot, but preserve their capture/session clocks and declared angle bindings.
Treat an edited reference as a separate discontinuous clock. If sync is not
proven, retain the uncertainty and do not invent lip alignment.

Mixed recordings need the same care as per-person sources. Consume the public
speaker evidence and caller-authored name bindings when available. Anonymous
slots are generation-local; camera ownership does not identify an audible
speaker. Keep overlap and unknown coverage visible, and never use a nearest,
majority or voiceprint guess to manufacture a name. Raw-source ownership and
audible-speaker identity remain separate when bleed or off-camera speech exists.

## Sound, captions and picture

Measure dialogue clips before applying matching gains, then inspect the mix and
encoded delivery. A strict master target remains a requested postcondition; a
meter discrepancy is evidence to report, not permission to relax it. Use native
styled/timed captions when they meet the brief, or retain an external transparent
overlay with its exact frame clock, font and alpha recipe. Check captions at the
intended phone or player size.

Review each changed join with context on both sides, including room tone and
speaker handoffs. Re-transcription can find defects but abrupt isolated snippets
can bias its edge words; use padded or neighboring windows, alignment or other
independent evidence and keep more audio when checks disagree. Verify framing,
face coverage, exposure, clipping, transition motion and the ending in the
delivered render. No human listening, labeling or watching is a required gate.
