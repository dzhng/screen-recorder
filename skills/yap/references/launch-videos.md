# Launch and demo videos

Read this reference when the brief is to launch a product, announce a feature,
or demonstrate a workflow. It owns the story shape for launch work; operation
contracts, caption construction, picture checks and audio finishing stay in the
focused references linked by [video-use-cases](video-use-cases.md).

## Start with the capability question

Before touching media, ask: **what tools, skills, models and asset libraries are
available beyond the Yap skill and product?** Record only capabilities relevant
to this brief, with provider, readiness and provenance, in the task workspace.
Use what is already available. Recommend a missing external capability only when
the requested result materially needs it; never install a generator, music
source, account or model as a default. Prepare registered first-class Yap models
through the product's own operation when the workflow needs them.

## Shape one promise

Choose one promise and make every beat prove it:

`hook → problem or stakes → visible proof → benefit → next action`

Open with the strongest result, recognizable friction or surprising claim. Show
the action and its consequence together so the viewer can understand cause and
effect. Cut feature lists that do not advance the promise. End on the benefit and
the truthful action supplied by the brief. A launch teaser may withhold the
answer; a promised demo must show its payoff.

When the brief asks for multiple styles, branch from one checked assembly and
record each branch's reference, pacing, grade, music and graphics decisions.
Do not turn a style word such as “Hollywood” into letterbox bars, a crop or a
different aspect ratio without an explicit request. Preserve the full picture
while choosing production value through pacing, framing, sound, captions and
transitions.

## Study references instead of guessing their mood

Inspect a named reference's actual structure, frame treatment, caption behavior,
transitions and audio. If the requested music or reference audio is available,
measure and audition it against speech; do not infer a genre from its name.
Retain imported or generated external assets with their bytes, hashes and recipe
so a clean workspace can reproduce the branch. Keep source media outside project
Git and keep the brief, decisions and receipts in the project collection.

Use raw camera sources when they support the requested shot and their clock or
angle relationship is proven. An edited reference is discontinuous evidence, not
an automatic sync source. Use explicit face/frame evidence to keep the subject
inside the requested geometry, and check exposure, clipping, readable captions
and content preservation on delivered frames.

## Finish and deliver

Choose captions, graphics, music and effects for the promise and viewing size.
Native Yap primitives are preferred when they meet the brief; an external alpha
overlay or asset is an explicit interchange path, not a hidden fallback. Match
dialogue levels before mastering the mix, then verify the encoded delivery rather
than weakening a strict target to make a render pass.

Review the opening, every changed join, the ending and representative middle in
the rendered candidate. Repair observed defects within a bounded budget, rerun
the changed checks and record any remaining uncertainty. The user gives the
direction; watching or listening by the user is never a required QA step.
