# Teasers, trailers and episode cold opens

Read this reference when the brief is to tease a launch, trailer, social promo
or episode introduction. It owns open-loop story choices; operation contracts,
caption design, transition checks and acoustic verification remain in the
focused references linked by [video-use-cases](video-use-cases.md).

## Start with the capability question

Before editing, ask: **what tools, skills, models and asset libraries are
available beyond the Yap skill and product?** Save only the relevant providers,
readiness and provenance. Use what is available; recommend a missing external
capability only when this brief materially needs it. Do not install external
tools, accounts or generators by default. Prepare registered Yap models through
their advertised operation when required.

## Build one honest curiosity loop

Inspect the full interview or episode before selecting a teaser. Establish only
the context needed for one question, tension or reveal to land, then point to a
truthful destination supplied by the brief. A teaser may end on an open loop; a
full highlight must preserve the answer and its qualifications.

### The question-end strategy

When the source is a full 4:4 interview or the teaser is a dark episode
introduction, a useful strategy is to cut **immediately after a complete,
provocative question and before the answer starts**. A spicy question can be the
climax. Preserve its final syllable and inflection; do not chop the question to
make the timing. Keep the withheld answer out of captions, titles, thumbnails,
narration and reaction graphics.

This is optional, not a universal template. If the brief promises an answer,
include the answer and the qualifications that make it intelligible. Do not
splice a reaction from another exchange so a guest appears to endorse a claim
they did not answer. A pause, music sting, partial reveal or end card may sharpen
the loop only when it does not invent context or imply a false response.

## Reference-conditioned treatment

Study the actual reference's pacing, cut density, audio energy, grade, captions,
transitions and graphics. Do not translate “spicy,” “Hollywood” or “dark” into a
fixed preset. Preserve full content unless the brief authorizes a crop or bars;
use framing and pacing to create intensity while checking faces, text and
exposure. External music, SFX, generated graphics or alpha overlays are explicit
assets: retain their bytes, hashes, source/recipe and timing so the teaser can be
replayed without hidden downloads.

## Verify the withheld beat

Review the complete question in context and inspect the first answer syllable on
the source and rendered clocks. Use waveform/spectrogram evidence, padded-window
re-transcription and alignment where available; a lone abrupt snippet is not
proof of a clipped or intact word. Check that no answer leaks through a caption,
thumbnail, title, music cue or reaction shot. Review the opening, question-end,
transition and destination card in the delivered render, repair observed defects
within a bounded budget and record residual uncertainty without asking the user
to perform QA.
